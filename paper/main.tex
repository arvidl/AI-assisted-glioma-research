\begin{abstract}
Glioma research must connect anatomical tumour burden with the cells, circuits
and networks that occupy the same brain, and with the cognition of the patient.
This perspective is organised around twelve executable notebooks, each pairing
one clinical or biological question with code, controls and an explicit
evidence label. The examples run from image to cognition: measuring tumour
compartments and recomputing a published segmentation cohort; following one
openly released glioblastoma over serial scans with a growth model whose
failure is diagnosed and revised; constructed models of growth, cell-state
composition, neuron--glioma feedback, astrocytic potassium clearance and
extracellular fields; a synthetic network lesioned by a tissue-guided tumour;
one patient's pre- and post-operative connectomes compared with a virtual
resection; and cognitive test scores from an open brain-tumour cohort,
including an audit of an AI-generated summary slide. Three kinds of AI are kept
apart: trained imaging models, generative research assistants that helped
write the code and text, and conventional numerical solvers that reproduce
specified equations without a language-model call. A final example shows how
records and independent checks make an assisted contribution inspectable, and
why passing checks is not evidence of scientific quality or benefit. Synthetic
constructions, real-data observations, published results, hypotheses and
proposed studies are labelled separately. No model here is calibrated to a
patient, no clinical validation is claimed, and the comparative benefit of AI
assistance is not measured.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} glioma; MRI; tumour growth models; cancer
neuroscience; connectome; cognition; AI assistance; reproducibility

\section{Introduction}

The value of a computational glioma figure depends on the question it answers.
A segmentation may define a region for measurement, an equation may construct
a trajectory, a connectome may suggest which connections a tumour interrupts,
and a cognitive table may describe how a patient performed. Each can be useful
without supporting a treatment conclusion.

Three kinds of AI and computation appear in this work and are kept apart. An
image-analysis model, such as one developed with nnU-Net, maps an image to
labels and requires validation of that mapping~\cite{isensee2021}. A generative
research assistant can help formulate questions, write code, inspect artifacts
and revise prose. A numerical solver advances a specified equation. The solver
is not an imaging model, and the correctness of its update does not validate
the assistant that helped implement it. Rebuilding every figure in this paper
runs conventional code only and calls no language model.

The paper accompanies a public repository of twelve notebooks
(Table~\ref{tab:notebooks}). Each notebook asks one question, calls a shared
Python package, and states what kind of evidence it supplies. We use five
evidence labels throughout:
\evidence{synthetic} (a construction whose inputs are prescribed),
\evidence{published} (a result reported by others, quoted or recomputed from
released files), \evidence{observation} (real data, described without a
fitted model or causal claim), \evidence{hypothesis} (a proposed mechanism not
tested here) and \evidence{proposed} (a study design, not carried out). Every
section below opens with a short block that states the question, the AI
contribution, the investigator's choices, the verification and the evidence
status.

The sections follow a path from image to cognition. Section~\ref{sec:measure}
measures and locates a tumour; Section~\ref{sec:lumiere} follows one patient's
serial scans; Section~\ref{sec:growth} turns to growth and its cellular
environment; Section~\ref{sec:physiology} to physiology at the glioma--brain
interface; Section~\ref{sec:networks} to networks and cognition; and
Section~\ref{sec:audit} to auditing the AI-assisted work itself.

The construction is an ellipsoid on a $1.2\times1.2\times2.0$\,mm grid with
nested compartments in BraTS label numbering: 1 for necrosis or non-enhancing
tumour, 2 for oedema or FLAIR abnormality and 4 for enhancing tumour, arranged
as in a typical glioblastoma. These are prescribed regions, not observed
tissue. For a binary region $M$ and a voxel-to-world affine $A$ in
millimetres,
\begin{equation}
 V(M)=\frac{|M|\,|\det A_{1:3,1:3}|}{1000}\quad\mathrm{mL}.
\end{equation}
The determinant handles rotation, reflection and shear. The NIfTI reader
requires a coded transform, established units and finite integer labels, and
rejects conflicting qform and sform transforms. Comparisons require the same
physical grid; the code never registers or resamples silently.

A four-voxel translation is compared with a 12\% increase in each semi-axis.

\section{Growth and its environment}
\label{sec:growth}

The examples in this and the next section resolve different quantities on
different timescales. They are not parts of one calibrated multiscale
simulator: seconds-scale extracellular potassium, a static potential profile
and a day-scale density are not interchangeable.

\subsection{A checked proliferation--invasion model (notebook 02)}
\label{sec:dynamics}

\aicontribution
{How do migration, local proliferation and the choice of boundary change a simulated tumour?}
{Research assistance adapting solver interfaces, controls, the tissue phantom and explanation.}
{Tumour dynamics was requested; coefficients, geometry and boundaries are prescribed.}
{No-change and single-process controls, conservation without growth, analytic solutions and step constraints.}
{\evidence{synthetic}; no fitted patient trajectory.}

Serial MRI observes a glioma through boundaries, each delineated with error.
Whether such observations could support forecasts of growth has been framed
by analogy with numerical weather prediction~\cite{yankeelov2015}. Weather
forecasting now combines physics-based ensembles with machine-learned models
trained on decades of dense observations~\cite{price2025,bodnar2025,rasp2026}.
A glioma is seen in a few scans months apart, its course is altered by
treatment, and pseudoprogression can mimic growth~\cite{wen2023}.

The baseline advances a normalised density $c$:
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial c}{\partial t}=\nabla\cdot(D\nabla c)+\rho c(1-c),
\label{eq:dynamics}
\end{equation}
the Fisher--Kolmogorov--Petrovsky--Piskunov equation, whose fronts travel at
$2\sqrt{D\rho}$~\cite{fisher1937,kpp1937,murray2003}. Swanson, Alvord and
Murray used the proliferation--invasion form with faster migration in white
than grey matter~\cite{swanson2000,swanson2004}. $D$ is in mm$^2$/day, $\rho$
per day and coordinates in millimetres; the boundary has no flux, and steps
outside the diffusion stability bound are rejected rather than clipped.
\IfFileExists{generated/numbers.tex}{

covers \ExampleVisibleArea{}\,mm$^2$ at threshold 0.5 and
\ExampleLowArea{}\,mm$^2$ at 0.05 after 20 days; doubling $D$ gives
\ExampleAlternativeArea{}\,mm$^2$ at 0.5 (Figure~\ref{fig:dynamics}).
}{\draftnotice{Run make paper to generate the dynamics values.}}
The same state looks larger or smaller depending on the threshold, and the
integrated density can grow while a high-threshold area shrinks.

Notebook~02 then places a left parietal seed on a constructed coronal phantom
with grey matter, white matter, CSF and prescribed fibre directions. Faster
migration in white matter and along fibres steers the simulated tumour across
the corpus callosum. In a recent analysis of recurrence sites in 195 patients
with grade~4 glioma, the authors suggest that posterior white matter, including
the splenium, may facilitate migration to new lobes~\cite{hannisdal2026}.
Notebook~11 reuses this phantom and tumour.

\label{sec:niche}

Macrophage-associated signalling can drive mesenchymal-like (MES-like)
glioblastoma state transitions in experimental systems~\cite{hara2021,hara2025correction},

ecosystem~\cite{wang2024}. Let $P$ aggregate other tumour states and $M$ a

\begin{equation}
 \frac{dM}{dt}=k_f sP-k_rM,\qquad \frac{dP}{dt}=-\frac{dM}{dt},
 \label{eq:niche}
\end{equation}
which conserves $P+M$ locally. The cue is neither a cell count nor a cytokine
concentration, and no rate is inferred from the cited experiments.

\subsection{A neuron--glioma feedback experiment (notebook 06)}
\label{sec:feedback}

\aicontribution
{Does a glioma feed its own growth, and can a growth curve tell?}
{Research assistance implementing the model, nested controls and sensitivity.}
{Cancer neuroscience was requested; the slow index and coefficients are prescribed choices.}
{Uncoupled limit, analytic constant-signal case, conservation, refinement and growth confounding.}
{\evidence{synthetic}; no fitted physiology or cognitive endpoint.}

Cancer neuroscience connects neurosurgery and tumour biology~\cite{winkler2023,monje2025}.
Neuron--glioma synapses and electrical integration have experimental
support~\cite{venkatesh2019,venkataramani2019}; their plasticity is regulated by
BDNF--TrkB signalling~\cite{taylor2023}; human cortical-tissue experiments link
neuronal excitability to glioma proliferation~\cite{mcalpine2026}; and tumour
functional connectivity is associated with cognition and survival in
patients~\cite{krishna2023}. Tumour microtubes add a cellular network
level~\cite{osswald2015,weil2017,venkataramani2022,hausmann2023}, estimated in
patient samples through a transcriptional signature~\cite{hai2024}; they are
far below MRI resolution.

The construction adds a slow, dimensionless growth-signal index $a$ to the
density $c$:
\begin{align}
 \frac{\partial c}{\partial t}&=\nabla\cdot(D\nabla c)+\rho(1+\kappa a)c(1-c),\\
 \frac{\partial a}{\partial t}&=\frac{b-a}{\tau}+\beta c(1-a),
 \label{eq:neural}
\end{align}
with prescribed drive $b$, coupling $\kappa$, feedback rate $\beta$ per day
and relaxation time $\tau$ in days. The index is not voltage, firing rate, EEG
or synapse count. Three nested runs share all other inputs: baseline
($\kappa=\beta=0$), one-way ($\kappa=1.5$, $\beta=0$) and feedback
($\kappa=1.5$, $\beta=0.12$/day).

\subsection{Astrocytic potassium clearance (notebook 07)}
\label{sec:potassium}

\aicontribution
{When the same activity releases the same potassium, what changes if astrocytic clearance slows?}
{Codex helped formulate and implement the excess-K and Nernst example and its checks.}
{The investigator requested astrocytes; volume, rates, pulses and concentrations are prescribed choices.}
{Independent analytic solution, concentration balance, limiting cases, clearance ambiguity and Nernst limits.}
{\evidence{synthetic}; scientific review pending.}

Astrocytes regulate the extracellular environment; glial-conditional Kir4.1
perturbation links astroglial potassium clearance to short-term synaptic
responses~\cite{sibille2014}. Astrocyte--glioblastoma connections also have
metabolic consequences, including GAP43-dependent mitochondrial
transfer~\cite{watson2023}, a process distinct from potassium clearance.
Notebook~07 uses one compartment of excess extracellular potassium $k$ (mM)
above a 3\,mM reference:
\begin{equation}
 \frac{dk}{dt}=q(t)-(\lambda_a+\lambda_o)k,
 \label{eq:potassium}
\end{equation}
with prescribed release $q$ (mM/s) and lumped astrocytic and other clearance
rates (s$^{-1}$). Release is 0.6\,mM/s in two four-second pulses; other
clearance is 0.1\,s$^{-1}$ and astrocytic clearance 0.8, 0.2 or 0\,s$^{-1}$.
The ideal K equilibrium potential uses 140\,mM intracellular potassium at
37\,$^\circ$C, $E_K=(RT/F)\ln([K]_{out}/[K]_{in})$.
\IfFileExists{generated/numbers.tex}{
Peak excess potassium is \ExampleKReferencePeak{} and \ExampleKReducedPeak{}\,mM
for reference and reduced astrocytic clearance, shifting $E_K$ by up to
\ExampleKReferenceShift{} and \ExampleKReducedShift{}\,mV
(Figure~\ref{fig:potassium}).
}{}
The trajectory depends only on $\lambda_a+\lambda_o$, so a potassium
measurement alone cannot attribute clearance to astrocytes within this model;
selective perturbation would be needed. $E_K$ is not a simulated membrane
voltage or a cognitive endpoint.

\subsection{Potential is not field (notebook 08)}
\label{sec:lfp}

\aicontribution
{Does a recorded potential identify a spatial field or a cognitive mechanism?}
{Research assistance with the spatial derivative, controlled examples and evidence map.}
{The investigator requested LFPs and cognition; profiles and sampling are prescribed teaching choices.}
{Analytic derivative, sign, V/m conversion, offset invariance and primary-source scope.}

Local field potentials record extracellular potential relative to a
reference; synaptic currents, cellular geometry, synchrony and volume
conduction contribute~\cite{buzsaki2012}. A potential in volts and its
gradient in volts per metre are different quantities,
$\mathbf E=-\nabla\phi$. Weak extracellular fields can influence membrane
potential and spike timing in cortical slices~\cite{anastassiou2011}.
Notebook~08 prescribes two static potentials of equal 100\,$\mu$V amplitude
with wavelengths 10 and 2.5\,mm.

(Figure~\ref{fig:lfp}):
}{}
equal amplitudes, or a single voltage trace, do not determine the field. A
real estimate needs spatial sampling, reference and noise treatment and a
source model.

\subsection{From networks to cognition: hypotheses and a proposed study}

Cognition belongs in the research agenda as an explicitly assessed outcome,
not a label on a simulated quantity. Miller, Brincat and Roy propose a hybrid
synapse/wave account in which rhythmic fields organise context-dependent
activity~\cite{miller2026}. Asking how glioma-related perturbations of such
dynamics might affect cognition beyond anatomical burden is our
\evidence{hypothesis}; testing it needs task-related EEG, MEG or intracranial
recordings, not BOLD or behavioural scores alone.

We propose (\evidence{proposed}) asking whether pre-operative network and
dynamics information adds predictive value beyond baseline cognition,
clinical information and reviewed anatomy, with follow-up spatial span as a
candidate primary endpoint. A persistence rule is the first comparator,
followed by clinical, anatomical and then a few network or independently
fitted dynamics features, all in the same compact regularised model family.
Predictors must exist before surgery; preprocessing belongs inside training
folds; all visits of one person stay together; and errors are reported in
native units. Earlier virtual-resection work showed variable agreement with
post-operative functional connectivity~\cite{aerts2020}, and the single
patient of Section~\ref{sec:connectome} agrees no better. A larger, linked
glioma cohort is required.

A record validator checks form, not truth. It refuses a missing date, a
template placeholder, an empty list of checks, or a decision of
\emph{accepted} without a named reviewer; it cannot tell whether the checks
were run, whether they are adequate, or whether a named reviewer reviewed
anything.

Benefit is a separate question. A proposed pilot (\evidence{proposed})
separates productivity, scientific quality and clinical relevance: two
investigators each complete six matched task pairs, one version of each with
a specified generative assistant and the other without, in counterbalanced
order. The primary measure is quality-qualified completion time with a
60-minute cap; independent reviewers blinded to condition rate units,
correctness, controls, provenance and evidence boundaries. No session has
been run, and the design cannot establish general superiority.

\section{Discussion}

The contribution of these examples is a chain another researcher can
inspect: question, specification, contribution, check, output and the
boundary of what the output supports. The notebooks call one package, the
paper's numbers and figures come from the same functions, and the release
assets that hold licensed inputs are pinned by hash.

Three real-data examples show how narrow the supported conclusions are. The
cohort recomputation confirms the published overlap and adds a systematic
volume bias that overlap alone hides. The LUMIERE case shows that a model can
be revised for stated biological reasons and still not identify its
coefficients from one interval, while part of the later lesion lies outside
any local model. The sub-PAT16 connectome shows that the simplest network
chain, from tumour to lost connections to model functional connectivity,
does not describe one patient's measurements, with the upstream quality
control still unreviewed. The CANTAB example shows that one patient's change
can hinge on the choice of index and on one reference person, and that an
AI-generated summary of correct numbers can still be wrong.

The constructions serve a different purpose. They make assumptions visible
and testable: a threshold that changes apparent size, a composition that
changes without growth, a feedback that a growth curve cannot distinguish
from modulation, a clearance that a potassium trace cannot attribute, and a
potential that does not determine a field. None is calibrated, and none
predicts cognition.

The limitations are substantial. Synthetic shapes omit biological,
acquisition and treatment variability. The growth models have prescribed or
in-sample coefficients and no treatment term. The network model is linear
and has no haemodynamics. The real-data examples are one patient each, or a
small mixed cohort. The analog-cognition and cytoelectric interpretations
remain hypotheses. The records of AI assistance are retrospective summaries;
none of these artifacts measures the comparative benefit of AI assistance,
and no clinical utility is claimed.

\section{Conclusion}
Glioma research spans images, cells, circuits, networks and cognition, and
each level answers a different question. Twelve executable notebooks keep
their units, assumptions, evidence labels and limits explicit, from
segmentation agreement to one patient's connectome and cognitive scores. AI
supports these artifacts in three distinct roles. Records and checks that
could fail make an assisted contribution inspectable; scientific review,
comparative benefit and clinical utility remain to be established.
